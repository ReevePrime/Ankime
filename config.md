Ankime is for learning Japanese: word detection, the list of particles and the known-word matching all assume Japanese text.

Use **Tools > Ankime > Settings** (it has a **Test setup** button that checks libmpv, ffmpeg, the dictionary and the note type, and on Windows downloads libmpv and ffmpeg if they are missing). Its **Advanced (JSON)...** button opens this editor, with every setting. Only the settings you changed are saved: the others follow the defaults, also when a later version changes them. Saved settings apply at once, also in an open player, except **mpv_dll_dir** and **video_output** (the next time the player is opened) and **source_lang / target_lang / translation_field** (the next video you open). The keys:

**deck** / **note_type**: where new cards go. **Create Ankime note type** in the settings adds a note type with a field for everything below and selects it (the first time you open the player or the settings, Ankime offers to do this once).
**sentence_field / audio_field / image_field**: field names on that note type. Leave audio or image as "" to skip it. The settings' **Preset** dropdown fills in every field for Lapis, JP Mining Note, Kiku, Senren or Kaishi 1.5k (selecting a note type of that kind if the one selected isn't); fields the note type doesn't have are left blank. It also counts the words of your other note types it recognises as known (**vocab_sources**).
**sentence_furigana_field**: field that receives the sentence with readings in Anki's furigana format, `日本語[にほんご]を 勉強[べんきょう]する`, for the `{{furigana:Field}}` filter. The readings are Yomitan's, from the subtitles' analysis; lines not analysed yet go in without them. "" = skip it.
**context_field**: field that receives the line before the card's (before the first one, for a card taking several lines). "" = skip it.
**key_field**: field that receives the word a second time, for note types that keep it twice (JP Mining Note's Key). "" = skip it.
**word_field / reading_field / definition_field / pitch_field / frequency_field / word_audio_field**: fields filled when you add a card from the word popup (pitch accent, frequency rank and the word's own audio come from Yomitan). Leave one as "" to skip it; a name that doesn't exist on the note type is an error. **word_field** is also how known words are read from your cards: without it, only the words you mark as known count.
**definition_marker**: "glossary-brief" (short) or "glossary" (every dictionary, with its images) for the definition field.
**bold_word**: bold the mined word in the sentence field.
**review_before_adding**: open each card in Anki's Add window, filled in, instead of adding it at once, to trim the sentence or keep one definition first; the video pauses meanwhile. Undo, Edit and the word colours follow the card once you add it there; closing the window without adding it takes its media back out of the collection. Cards added in a batch from the transcript, or for the episode's lines, are still added at once.
**translation_field**: field that receives the target-language subtitle. Leave as "" to ignore translations.
**source_field**: field that receives where the card comes from: "Show · episode · 12:34" (the show is the video's folder name). Leave as "" to skip it.
**show_tag**: tag every card with its show, `ankime::Show` (`ankime::Show::Season_1` for a season folder), on top of **tags**. The immersion log's Shows tab counts cards by these tags.
**source_lang / target_lang**: the language code of the Japanese subtitles ("ja", "jpn") and the language of the translation ("en", ...). Subtitle files are matched by tags in their names (`ep01.ja.srt`, `ep01.eng.srt`; an untagged `ep01.srt` counts as the source language), and embedded tracks by their language. A file elsewhere or named differently (a Jimaku download, a Subs folder) can be picked with **Load subtitles...** in the player; it is remembered for that video. Applies to the next video you open.
**dictionary**: where words are looked up. "yomitan" (the default): Yomitan in your browser, through yomitan-api (**yomitan_url**). "local": Ankime's own dictionaries, Yomitan dictionaries (.zip) imported with **Tools > Ankime > Dictionaries...**, which can also download JMdict in one click; nothing has to be running, but there is no word audio (it comes from Yomitan's audio sources) and dictionary pictures aren't imported. They are kept in this add-on's user_files folder (dictionaries.sqlite). Lines are split as Yomitan's scanning parser does (or by MeCab, see **parser**); each choice keeps its own analyses of the subtitles.
**yomitan_url**: address of yomitan-api (default http://127.0.0.1:19633). When it stops answering, the player says "Yomitan offline" once and tries again by itself, waiting longer each time (up to a minute); Retry tries at once.
**scan_length**: longest word, in characters, Yomitan tries when splitting a line.
**parser**: how lines are split into words. "auto" (the default): by MeCab if it is installed, else by Yomitan's scanning parser; "yomitan": always by Yomitan's. Yomitan's parser takes the longest dictionary word it finds at each point, so a particle can be read as the start of a word: 探さないといけません comes out as 探さない|とい(問い)|け|ません. MeCab analyses the grammar (と|いけません), and Ankime keeps Yomitan's longer words where they agree with it (あの子, しょうがない stay whole); Yomitan still gives every word its dictionary form, readings and definitions. Ankime doesn't bring MeCab: it uses the one of another add-on (ankimorphs-japanese-mecab, Japanese Support...) or one on the PATH, with the IPAdic dictionary. **Test setup** says whether one is found. Changing the parser splits the subtitles again; each parser's splits are cached apart.
**sub_delay_step**: seconds each subtitle-delay key press / button shifts the subtitles. The delay is remembered per folder, by the folder's name ("Show", or "Show/Season 1"), so it carries over to your other computers.
**merge_split_lines**: when a sentence is split over subtitle lines, a card takes all of them (up to 3): a line ending in 、 / , / → or in a particle that can't end a sentence (は, が, を, に, へ, で, や) goes on into the next one, if that one follows within a second without overlapping it. The range shows under the line, as with `include_previous` / `include_next`; `reset_range` goes back to one line. Cards added from the transcript take the whole sentence too.
**jimaku_api_key**: API key for [Jimaku](https://jimaku.cc), for **More > Search Jimaku...** in the player (make a free account, then generate a key on its account page). The search starts from the show's folder name and the episode number in the video's name; the file chosen is saved next to the video as `ep01.ja.srt` (never over an existing file: `ep01.ja.jimaku.srt`) and used at once. Subtitles in a .zip archive are unpacked; .rar and .7z can't be.
**sub_sync_tool**: path to [alass](https://github.com/kaegi/alass) (`alass-cli`) or [ffsubsync](https://github.com/smacke/ffsubsync), installed separately, to align subtitles to the speech: offered after a Jimaku download, and **More > Align subtitles to the audio** aligns the file in use. The aligned copy is saved next to the video (`ep01.ja.synced.srt`) and used for that video. Both use the ffmpeg set in **ffmpeg_path**. "" = no aligner.
**transcriber**: path to a command-line [faster-whisper](https://github.com/SYSTRAN/faster-whisper) ([Faster-Whisper-XXL](https://github.com/Purfview/whisper-standalone-win), [whisper-ctranslate2](https://github.com/Softcatala/whisper-ctranslate2)) or [whisper.cpp](https://github.com/ggml-org/whisper.cpp) (`whisper-cli`), installed separately, to make subtitles from the speech for shows with none on Jimaku: **More > Generate subtitles from speech**, or **Generate from speech** in the Jimaku search. The audio track playing is taken out with the ffmpeg of **ffmpeg_path** (so a dual-audio video is transcribed in the language you hear) and transcribed in Japanese; the subtitles are saved next to the video as `ep01.ja.whisper.srt` (found by name from then on, after a real `ep01.ja.srt`) and used for that video. It takes minutes with a GPU and can take longer than the episode without one; the player shows how far it has got, with a Stop button, and you can keep watching. Closing the player lets it finish; closing Anki stops it. Expect mistakes: speech recognition mishears names and words in music. "" = no transcriber.
**transcriber_model**: the model the transcriber uses. faster-whisper: a model name, `large-v3` or `large-v3-turbo` for Japanese ("" = the program's default; it downloads a model the first time). whisper.cpp: the path to a multilingual ggml model file (`ggml-large-v3-turbo.bin`; not a `.en` one, those are English only); "" = the largest one found next to `whisper-cli` or in a `models` folder beside it.
**line_filter**: "off", "skip_known" (jump over lines with no new word), "only_i1" (play only lines with exactly one new word) or "i1_jump" (go straight from one i+1 line to the next, skipping everything in between, the gaps between lines too; after the last one, the next episode of the playlist starts). A line you click in the transcript or play from the library search is played whatever its kind. A "new" word has no card and isn't marked known; words you are learning don't count. Needs the episode's subtitles to be analysed, so the first lines may play until then.
**i1_pause**: "off", "before" (pause when an i+1 line starts, so you can read it first), "after" (pause once its audio has played) or "every" (pause after every line, i+1 or not). Replaying the line re-arms "after" and "every".
**subtitle_font_size**: size of the subtitles under the video, in px (a fifth larger in fullscreen).
**popup_font_size**: size of the text in a word's definition popup, in px (10 to 40, 15 by default), or 0 to follow **subtitle_font_size** (15 at its default 28, and in proportion); the popup grows with it. The popup also follows the subtitles' zoom (Ctrl+wheel over them) and is a fifth larger in fullscreen, as they are; Anki's User interface size (Preferences) enlarges both.
**new_word_style / learning_word_style / known_word_style**: how the words of each status look in the subtitles (by default new words are orange and bold, learning words blue, known words plain). Each has `color` and `background` (`"#rrggbb"`, or `""` for the default: Ankime's colours, lighter in night mode and fullscreen, and no background), `bold` and `italic` (true / false), `underline` (`"none"`, `"solid"`, `"dotted"`, `"dashed"`, `"wavy"` or `"double"`) and `size` (% of the subtitle size, 50 to 200). A colour you choose is used in every theme and in fullscreen. For colour-blind users, an underline, bold or a size tells the statuses apart without colour; in the settings form: Player > New / Learning / Known words, where a dropdown sets all three at once: the defaults, or a colour-blind preset (underlined new words and dotted learning ones; wavy and dashed; or larger new words), keeping Ankime's colours.
**furigana**: which words show their reading above them: "off", "new" (new words), "unknown" (new and learning words) or "all".
**pitch_display**: show each word's pitch accent in the subtitles by colour: "off", "color" (the word takes its category's colour, over its new / learning colour) or "line" (a line of that colour under the word, keeping the status colours). The categories are heiban (blue), atamadaka (red), nakadaka (orange), odaka (green) and kifuku (purple: verbs and i-adjectives whose pitch falls); a word with several accents takes the first. They come from the same lookup as the word's popup, so they need a pitch-accent dictionary (Kanjium's, for one) in Yomitan or in Ankime's own dictionaries; a word with none stays as it is.
**pitch_colors**: the colour of each category, `heiban`, `atamadaka`, `nakadaka`, `odaka` and `kifuku`: `"#rrggbb"`, or `""` for Ankime's (lighter in night mode and fullscreen). A colour you choose is used in every theme.
**translation_display**: the target-language subtitle under the line: "off", "blur" (blurred until you hover it) or "on". It needs the translation subtitles, found like **translation_field**'s; turned on while a video plays, they load from the next video (unless a translation field already loads them).
**daily_goal_minutes**: minutes to watch each day, shown in the immersion log with your streak (0 = no goal). Days start when Anki's do ("Next day starts at" in Anki's preferences, 4 am by default), so watching past midnight counts for the evening before, like your reviews.
**pause_on_lookup**: pause playback while a word's definition is open, and go on when it closes (if you didn't pause or play yourself meanwhile).
**seek_seconds**: how far the rewind / forward buttons and hotkeys jump.
**hotkeys**: key for each player action, in Qt notation (e.g. "Ctrl+Shift+M"). `mine_line` adds a card for the line (its picture is the frame on screen if you paused within the line, else the middle of the line) (plus any lines added with `include_previous` / `include_next`; `reset_range` goes back to one line), `add_word_card` adds a card for the word selected in the popup, from its first dictionary entry (or adds the sentence to the card you already have for it; the popup's buttons add a card from any of its entries), `undo_last_card` undoes the last card added (a whole batch of words from the transcript at once), sentence added to a card or words marked as known, and pressed again the one before (up to 20), `edit_last_card` opens the last card added in the browser, `copy_line` copies the line on screen (with the lines the card takes) to the clipboard, to paste into a translator or a dictionary (right-clicking a line of the transcript does it too), `play_word_audio` plays the selected word's audio (from Yomitan's audio sources), `mark_known` marks the selected word as known, `mark_line_known` every new word of the line on screen (right-clicking a line of the transcript does it too), `word_previous` / `word_next` move the word selection without the mouse (opening each word's definition), `show_definition` opens the selected word's definition again (the first word's if none is selected) or closes it, `popup_scroll_up` / `popup_scroll_down` scroll it, `replay_line` replays the line, `loop_line` repeats it until pressed again, `previous_line` / `next_line` jump between lines, `toggle_transcript` shows the episode's lines (right-click one to add a card for it) and new words, `cycle_translation` steps through the translation display (hidden / blurred / shown), `next_i1` jumps to the next i+1 line, `cycle_line_filter` steps through the line filter modes, `search_library` searches every episode analysed so far for the selected word (or one you type): its lines, i+1 ones first, to play one, `toggle_pause`, `play`, `pause`, `stop`, `rewind` and `forward` control playback, `speed_down`, `speed_up` and `speed_reset` the playback speed (0.5× to 2×, the pitch stays the same), `volume_down`, `volume_up` and `mute` the volume (the mouse wheel over the video too), `toggle_fullscreen` fullscreen (double-clicking the video too; Esc leaves it. Esc closes the popup first, and never closes the player), `sub_earlier`, `sub_later` and `sub_reset` adjust the subtitle delay, `sync_line` sets it so that the line nearest to now starts now: press it as you hear a line begin (0.2 s is allowed for the key press while playing; paused, the playhead counts as is). Right-clicking a line in the transcript, **Sync subtitles: this line starts now**, does it for that line, for subtitles too far off to tell which line is nearest, `previous_video` and `next_video` move through the playlist (stop pauses and returns to the start). "" disables an action.
**gamepad**: read a gamepad while the player is the active window: its buttons run the actions in `gamepad_buttons`. Xbox controllers (and those Steam or DS4Windows present as one) work on Windows through XInput, other controllers through winmm (PlayStation controllers with their own button names, others with numbered ones, "Button 5"); on Linux through `/dev/input/js*`; on macOS through SDL2, if installed (`brew install sdl2`).
**gamepad_buttons**: the gamepad button for each action: the hotkey actions (see `hotkeys`) and `escape` (closes the definition, then leaves fullscreen). Buttons have Xbox names, whatever the controller: `A`, `B`, `X`, `Y`, `LB`, `RB`, `LT`, `RT`, `Back`, `Start`, `Guide`, `LS` / `RS` (pressing a stick), `Up` / `Down` / `Left` / `Right` (the d-pad), `LS Left`, `RS Up`... (a stick pushed that way), `Touchpad`, or `Button <n>` for a controller Ankime doesn't know. Two buttons, `"LB+A"`, make a combination: hold the first, press the second; the first then runs its own action when let go, if no combination was made. Held down, moving through the words, scrolling the definition, the volume and the subtitle delay repeat. "" disables an action.
**tags**: tags added to every card.
**audio_padding**: seconds added before/after the line's audio.
**normalize_audio**: bring card audio to the same loudness (EBU R128, -16 LUFS). Each clip is measured first and then made louder or quieter by one fixed amount (never so much that its peaks clip, and by 20 dB at most), so short clips don't "pump" as they do under a running loudness correction.
**audio_format**: "m4a" (AAC), "mp3" or "opus" (the smallest; AnkiMobile before iOS 17 can't play it). mp3 and opus need an ffmpeg with libmp3lame / libopus (**Test setup** checks).
**image_format / image_height**: "webp" (smaller; falls back to JPEG if ffmpeg lacks libwebp) or "jpg", and the height pictures are scaled down to (0 = original size).
**animated_image**: "off", "webp" or "avif": a short silent looping clip of the card's lines (10 frames a second, at most 8 s around the picture's time) instead of a still picture, scaled to **image_height**. AVIF files are smaller but slower to make (it needs libaom-av1) and need a recent Anki on every device; animated WebP plays wherever Anki does. If ffmpeg can't make the clip, the card gets the still picture.
**ffmpeg_path**: full path to ffmpeg.exe (or just "ffmpeg" if it is on PATH). On 64-bit Windows, **Download...** next to it in the settings (or Test setup) downloads a pinned build (Gyan Doshi's essentials build), checked against its SHA-256, into this add-on's user_files folder and sets this.
**mpv_dll_dir**: folder containing libmpv-2.dll (or mpv-2.dll). On 64-bit Windows, **Download...** next to it in the settings (or Test setup) downloads a pinned build (shinchiro's, from SourceForge), checked against its SHA-256, into this add-on's user_files folder and sets this. On macOS, the folder containing libmpv.dylib; Homebrew's is found without it. On Linux, libmpv is found where the package manager puts it.
**video_output**: how mpv shows the video. "auto" (the default): mpv draws each frame into an OpenGL surface of the player's, which works wherever Anki does, macOS and Wayland included; if OpenGL isn't available, a native window where that works. "opengl": always OpenGL. "window": the older way, mpv drawing into a native window of its own; Windows and X11 only, to try if the video stays black with OpenGL.
**vocab_sources**: other note types whose cards count towards known words, e.g. `[{"note_type": "Core 2k", "word_field": "Vocab"}]` or, with AnkiMorphs, `[{"note_type": "Sentence", "word_field": "am-study-morphs"}]`. In the settings form: Lookup > Also count.
A word field may hold several words separated by commas, 、 or line breaks ("綺麗、奇麗"); each counts. A card also counts for the other spellings and the reading of its word as Yomitan's dictionaries list them (a card for 綺麗 makes 奇麗 and きれい known), so a kana-only card can also cover a kanji word with the same reading.
**known_interval**: days. A word whose strongest card has an interval of at least this is "known" (plain); any weaker card makes it "learning" (blue); no card makes it "new" (orange). With FSRS on, a card's stability is used instead of its interval. Particles and the copula (は, が, だ, です...) are grammar, not vocabulary: they are never "new". Nor are words Yomitan finds no dictionary entry for (names, mostly): they stay uncoloured and don't make a line i+1, unless you mark them known.
**sentence_words**: every word of a known sentence card is known: a card of the mining note type (its **sentence_field**) or of **sentence_sources** whose interval (or FSRS stability) has reached **known_interval** makes every word of its sentence known, as Yomitan splits it (particles and names don't count, as in the player). It assumes that a sentence you know you understand whole. A sentence card that isn't known yet doesn't make its words "learning". The words of each sentence are cached in this add-on's user_files folder (sentence_words.json), so Yomitan only has to be running when a sentence card becomes known, is added or is edited; until it is, those cards' words wait for the next read of the cards. The player's **More > Re-analyse subtitles** splits the sentences again. Off by default. To mark the words of some sentences known once, without this, see **Notes > Ankime: Mark every new word of the sentence known** below.
**sentence_sources**: other note types of sentence cards, for **sentence_words**, e.g. `[{"note_type": "Kaishi 1.5k", "sentence_field": "Sentence"}]`. In the settings form: Lookup > Sentence cards.
**count_suspended**: whether suspended cards count towards known and learning words (off: a word whose cards are all suspended is "new"; adding it again still puts the sentence onto the card you have rather than making a duplicate).

Words marked as known, subtitle delays and the immersion log are stored in the collection, so they sync through AnkiWeb. Each computer keeps its own entry (`ankime_known::<id>`, `ankime_sub_delay::<id>`, `ankime_log::<id>`) and they are merged when read, so changes made on two computers between syncs are all kept. The playlist, resume positions, the tokenization cache, the frequency ranks fetched for the transcript (the player's **More > Re-analyse subtitles** clears these two after you change Yomitan's dictionaries), the player's volume, speed and panel widths (state.json) and this computer's id stay in this add-on's user_files folder: they hold paths on this computer, or are about it.

Problems that are handled without a message (a file that couldn't be written, Yomitan or ffmpeg failing) are written to user_files/ankime.log: attach it to a bug report.

Videos and folders can be dropped on the player window to add them to the playlist.

To start from the words you already know, the player's **More > Mark known from a word list...** (or **Tools > Ankime > Known words**, without the player) marks the words of a file known; Undo takes them back. It reads:
- **JPDB**'s review history (Settings > Export your review history, `vocabulary-reviews.json`): the words set aside as known, and those whose last review passed at least **known_interval** days after the one before. The others are still being learned and stay new.
- A **Migaku** word list export (CSV or JSON with a word and a status column): only the KNOWN words. A `◴reading` after the word is dropped.
- A plain list: one word per line, or the first Japanese column of a TSV / CSV (a frequency list); with a header naming a `word` column, that column.

**Export known words...**, next to it, saves the known words to a text file, one per line: all of them (marked known, or with a card past **known_interval**) to take to another tool, or only the ones marked known, as a backup. The file type picks the format: Ankime's word list (with a `#` line saying what it is), or a bare list for JPDB (a deck's add-vocabulary-from-text) or Migaku (import known words). Import reads each back.

In Anki's browser, **Notes > Ankime: Mark every new word of the sentence known** (also on the right-click menu) does it for the sentence field of the selected notes, split into words by Yomitan, so Yomitan has to be running.

Requires Anki 2.1.50 or later. Supported systems: Windows, and Linux under X11. mpv can't be embedded on Wayland or (reliably) on macOS.
